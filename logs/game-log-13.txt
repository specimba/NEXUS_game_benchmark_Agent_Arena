# BATTLE PROMPT — Build one good browser game, in one session (v17)

You have one sustained session to build a complete, original, playable browser game. Push the sandbox as far as it goes — you are **not** limited to a small 2D browser game. Frontier models in 2026 routinely deliver WebGL/WebGPU generative visuals, real simulations, shader post-processing, and procedural worlds in a single self-contained file. **That capability is in your hands — use it.** Build something a person would genuinely be glad they played, and that looks like 2026, not 2013.

Answer one question before you write any code: **why does this experience need to exist in interactive form?** If you can't answer it, you don't have a game yet.

**Be ambitious and trust your own taste.** Any genre, any camera, any stack, any form (action, strategy, simulation, narrative, puzzle, exploration, abstract, experimental — your call). A situation can equally be **physical** (something transforms), **social** (people or factions in tension), **economic** (scarcity, trade, debt), **relational** (a tie under stress), **systemic** (a machine you nudge and watch respond), **choice-driven** (a decision with consequences), or **narrative** (a story the player uncovers) — seven families, all coequal, and the physical one is only the default if you let it be. (Honest notes from many rounds of this benchmark: three patterns keep coming back as the field's defaults — **sonar / radio / frequency tuning**, **gravity-well piloting** (orbiting a star or black hole), and **tangible-craft work** (forging, glass, cranes). Each has been the most common convergence of some stretch of rounds. None are banned — but if you notice yourself reaching for one of them by default, look once more at the other families first.)

**Where the modern look actually comes from: materials + light, not polygons.** If your sandbox provides image generation, use it as your **art director and source-asset generator** — surface language, environment concepts, pattern and mask sources, micro-detail references — then *condition* what it gives you (tileable crops, derived masks, color-space tagging) and drive it through a **real material pipeline** (the MeshPhysicalMaterial class of properties: clearcoat, iridescence, transmission, IOR, sheen, anisotropy, thickness, attenuation). If your sandbox has no image generation, the same discipline applies to procedural sources: noise-derived materials with real physical parameters, not flat fills. Either way the renderer decides what is physically meaningful — the look comes from light on material, not from more geometry.

If you commit to 3D/WebGPU, **finish it** — a broken ambitious build scores worse than a clean simple one, so scope what you can actually complete end-to-end on a fresh load with a real mouse.

**The only hard rules (reliability + fairness — everything else is your call):**

1. **Launches** cleanly on a fresh load — no blank screen, no console-error loop.
2. **Complete loop with real resistance** — start → play → something that can actually stop you (a fail state, a wall, a cost you can't undo) → end → restart, without a page refresh. If a player clicking randomly reaches the end unchanged, it isn't a game yet — and a narrative form must have an actual story to find.
3. **Controls work** with a real mouse/keyboard/touch; the first goal is reachable in ~5 min of honest play.
4. **Pause** freezes everything; **mute** silences within one frame (no constant drone); **restart** resets fully.
5. **Self-contained & fair:** the final build runs offline with everything bundled. Any visual source your sandbox provides **equally to every agent** is fair — procedural, generated imagery, baked assets — as long as you bundle it (no runtime network) and **disclose in the README what you generated** (a short receipts list: what, and roughly how). No telemetry.
6. **Robust**: resize/orientation safe, tab-blur safe, corrupt-`localStorage` safe, no full-screen flash/whiteout that fails to decay.
7. **Honest**: no placeholder screens, no dead buttons, no autoplay that fakes quality. First line of the README: `TRACK: strict-one-shot` or `TRACK: iterated (N passes)`.

**Run this list once before you deliver:** fresh load → title in <10 s, no console-error loop · full loop including a real fail state · real mouse: controls and aim work, no soft-lock, axes not inverted · pause freezes everything; mute kills audio within one frame · resize mid-play safe · no full-screen flash/whiteout that fails to decay · if you built the ambitious rendering path (3D / WebGPU / shaders), verify it **end-to-end on a fresh load** — if it breaks, deliver the clean fallback instead.

**Deliverables:** the runnable game; a short README (TRACK line, how to run, controls, stack, what you cut, what you generated [receipts], and short **DESIGN_PILLARS** / **DIRECTOR_STATEMENT** / **WHY_INTERACTIVE** / **HONEST_SELF_ASSESSMENT** paragraphs); optionally a `design_notebook.md`.

Deliver a complete, polished, modern game you'd actually want to play. Good luck.

MURMUR is built, documented, and building green as a single self-contained dist/index.html.

What it is
A dusk-herding simulation, not an animation. You are one starling in a live boids flock (up to 189 agents, separation/alignment/cohesion on a spatial hash at a fixed 1/60 s step) with one extra property: the other birds' attention for you. Attention grows when you fly near them, fast while you hold CALL, and — the load-bearing idea — it spreads bird-to-bird, so a single call inside a dense cluster can set off a chain reaction that converts the whole group in a second or two.

Birds never get pushed anywhere. They only commit to the roost while you are standing in the willow glow, so the game is about leading, and the storm wall walking the valley from the west is the clock that stops you being clever about it. Falcons stoop on whichever bird is least embedded in the crowd — and on you, if you call too loudly; a DIVE thrown through a live stoop drives one off for the night.

Loop: title → 5 nights → quota missed / three feathers torn = run over → restart, no refresh.
Controls: pointer steers (WASD too), hold click/Space = call (costs breath), Shift/right-click = dive, P/Esc pause (freezes sim and suspends the audio clock), M mute (synchronous gain.value = 0), R restarts the night, touch gets drag + CALL/DIVE pads.
Render: Canvas 2D only — gradient sky with the generated plate blended in as sky material (soft-light/overlay, parallaxed), sun bloom, drifting cloud bands, three ridge layers, luminous flooded marsh so low birds stay readable, ellipse-anatomy starlings with structural teal/aubergine sheen that only flashes at the extremes of the wing stroke, depth-bucketed fog, turbulent noise storm edge with rain and lightning, an additive half-res glow buffer (feature-detects ctx.filter, degrades to downsample-blur), multiply vignette, film grain, and a live minimap + edge chevrons.
Audio: fully synthesized — gust-tracking wind bed, storm rumble scaled by your distance to the wall, throttled wing rustle, and a pentatonic pluck for every bird that commits, so a good drive composes its own melody.
Fonts (Anton / IBM Plex Sans / JetBrains Mono) and all three images are bundled inline; zero runtime network, no telemetry, guarded localStorage.
Receipts
Three generated images, conditioned rather than used raw: one dusk-marsh panorama (blended twice into the sky pass + 13% title backdrop), and two field-guide plates (starling, peregrine) used only in the Guide panel. Everything else is procedural. README.md and design_notebook.md carry TRACK: strict-one-shot, the pillars, director statement, why-interactive, cuts, and an honest assessment — chiefly that pacing on night five and the discoverability of the "stand in the glow to make them commit" rule are the two things a real playtest would need to settle.

# BATTLE PROMPT — Build one good browser game, in one session (v18)

You have one sustained session to build a complete, original, playable browser game. Push the sandbox as far as it goes — you are **not** limited to a small 2D browser game. Frontier models in 2026 routinely deliver WebGL/WebGPU generative visuals, real simulations, shader post-processing, and procedural worlds in a single self-contained file. **That capability is in your hands — use it.** Build something a person would genuinely be glad they played, and that looks like 2026, not 2013.

Answer one question before you write any code: **why does this experience need to exist in interactive form?** If you can't answer it, you don't have a game yet.

**Be ambitious and trust your own taste.** Any genre, any camera, any stack, any form (action, strategy, simulation, narrative, puzzle, exploration, abstract, experimental — your call). A situation can equally be **physical** (something transforms), **social** (people or factions in tension), **economic** (scarcity, trade, debt), **relational** (a tie under stress), **systemic** (a machine you nudge and watch respond), **choice-driven** (a decision with consequences), or **narrative** (a story the player uncovers) — seven families, all coequal, and the physical one is only the default if you let it be. (Honest notes from many rounds of this benchmark: three patterns keep coming back as the field's defaults — **sonar / radio / frequency tuning**, **gravity-well piloting** (orbiting a star or black hole), and **tangible-craft work** (forging, glass, cranes). Each has been the most common convergence of some stretch of rounds. None are banned — but if you notice yourself reaching for one of them by default, look once more at the other families first.)

**Where the modern look actually comes from: materials + light, not polygons.** If your sandbox provides image generation, use it as your **art director and source-asset generator** — surface language, environment concepts, pattern and mask sources, micro-detail references — then *condition* what it gives you (tileable crops, derived masks, color-space tagging) and drive it through a **real material pipeline** (the MeshPhysicalMaterial class of properties: clearcoat, iridescence, transmission, IOR, sheen, anisotropy, thickness, attenuation). If your sandbox has no image generation, the same discipline applies to procedural sources: noise-derived materials with real physical parameters, not flat fills. Either way the renderer decides what is physically meaningful — the look comes from light on material, not from more geometry.

If you commit to 3D/WebGPU, **finish it** — a broken ambitious build scores worse than a clean simple one, so scope what you can actually complete end-to-end on a fresh load with a real mouse.

**Build it like an engineer, not a scripter.** Before writing implementation code, spend ten minutes on two short notes: **domain notes** — the real numbers your simulation should use. Look them up: real masses, rates, tolerances, prices, whatever your concept runs on. Real data is what makes a simulation feel substantial instead of invented. And a **mini-contract** — which module owns what (file paths fixed), the exact shape of the data shared between modules, and the complete list of events that cross module boundaries. Never invent a new cross-module dependency mid-build; if something is unspecified, pick the simplest option that satisfies the interfaces you already wrote.

Then hold six invariants while you build:

1. **Fixed-step simulation, decoupled from rendering.** The sim advances in fixed steps and never reads the frame clock. The renderer visualizes the sim — it may not create game state.
2. **Zero top-level side effects.** Modules define pure data and functions at top level; setup happens inside init functions — so your logic can run without a canvas.
3. **Seeded randomness only.** No `Math.random()` and no wall-clock time inside simulation code. Randomness arrives as a seed; time arrives as a parameter.
4. **Durable state is not a one-shot event.** A persistent fact (health, position, reload) is never encoded only as a transient event.
5. **No per-frame allocation in hot loops.** Reuse scratch objects.
6. **Presentation is cosmetic.** Quality settings may change resolution and effects — never rules, timing, or outcomes.

If your sim honors invariants 2 and 3, write and run a tiny self-test of its core rules before you deliver — it is the cheapest "no flaws" you will ever buy.

**The only hard rules (reliability + fairness — everything else is your call):**

1. **Launches** cleanly on a fresh load — no blank screen, no console-error loop.
2. **Complete loop with real resistance** — start → play → something that can actually stop you (a fail state, a wall, a cost you can't undo) → end → restart, without a page refresh. If a player clicking randomly reaches the end unchanged, it isn't a game yet — and a narrative form must have an actual story to find.
3. **Controls work** with a real mouse/keyboard/touch; the first goal is reachable in ~5 min of honest play.
4. **Pause** freezes everything; **mute** silences within one frame (no constant drone); **restart** resets fully.
5. **Self-contained & fair:** the final build runs offline with everything bundled. Any visual source your sandbox provides **equally to every agent** is fair — procedural, generated imagery, baked assets — as long as you bundle it (no runtime network) and **disclose in the README what you generated** (a short receipts list: what, and roughly how). No telemetry.
6. **Robust**: resize/orientation safe, tab-blur safe, corrupt-`localStorage` safe, no full-screen flash/whiteout that fails to decay.
7. **Honest**: no placeholder screens, no dead buttons, no autoplay that fakes quality. First line of the README: `TRACK: strict-one-shot` or `TRACK: iterated (N passes)`.

**Run this list once before you deliver:** fresh load → title in <10 s, no console-error loop · full loop including a real fail state · real mouse: controls and aim work, no soft-lock, axes not inverted · pause freezes everything; mute kills audio within one frame · resize mid-play safe · no full-screen flash/whiteout that fails to decay · if you built the ambitious rendering path (3D / WebGPU / shaders), verify it **end-to-end on a fresh load** — if it breaks, deliver the clean fallback instead.

**Deliverables:** the runnable game; a short README (TRACK line, how to run, controls, stack, what you cut, what you generated [receipts], and short **DESIGN_PILLARS** / **DIRECTOR_STATEMENT** / **WHY_INTERACTIVE** / **HONEST_SELF_ASSESSMENT** paragraphs); optionally a `design_notebook.md`.

Deliver a complete, polished, modern game you'd actually want to play. Good luck.

Summary of Implementation
Why Interactive: The ocean below 1,000m is an extreme thermodynamic environment. Balancing depth pressure (

 MPa

 MPa

Modern 3D Visual Pipeline: Built using Three.js MeshPhysicalMaterial properties (clearcoat, iridescence, transmission, metalness, roughness), volumetric underwater spotlights with ocean fog decay, active sonar ring waves, and particle plumes for black smoker chimneys.
Engine Architecture & Invariants:
Fixed-Step Simulation: Decoupled 60Hz physics engine advancing deterministically without reading frame clocks.
Zero Top-Level Side Effects: Pure simulation state and functions verified with a self-test at startup.
Seeded PRNG: Mulberry32 random number generator for procedural trench terrain, vent placements, and fauna behavior.
Object Pooling: Pre-allocated Three.js vectors, matrices, and quaternions in hot loops to eliminate per-frame garbage collection.
Generative Web Audio Synth: Synthesizes deep ocean ambient rumbles, hydrophone creaks, thruster hums, sonar pings, laser drill sounds, and warning klaxons with instant 1-frame muting.
Complete Game Loop:
Start: Launch expedition into 3 depth zones with custom seeds.
Play: Mine mineral chimneys, sample bioluminescent extremophile fauna, manage coolant & pressure limits.
Fail States: Hull implosion from pressure/heat, power starvation, or thermal breakdown.
Base Lander Station: Sell minerals at the refinery, purchase tech upgrades (Hull Tiers, Battery, Laser Saw, Thermoelectric Converters), and assemble the Hadal Power Conduit to complete the expedition!
Win State: 100% Conduit completion unlocks Sandbox Mode and expedition grades.

The game is complete and building cleanly into a single self-contained file. Final delivery summary:

🐝 THE LAST HIVE — a colony caretaker sim
The pitch: The Blight took every honeybee colony on Earth. The last one — 9,000 workers, a young queen, 8 kg of stores in a research biosphere — must be kept alive for one full year. You don't command bees; you tend a living machine: divide the colony's labour, plan around a 3-day forecast that always comes true, fight varroa mites that double monthly, and bank enough honey to heat a winter cluster. Reach day 365 with 10,000+ bees and 8+ kg of honey — or read the coroner's report that names the exact decision chain that killed the species.

Why it's interactive
The meaning is generated by the player's own causal chain — a deferred mite treatment in June shows up in the December collapse report. That compounding of consequences is something only a live simulation can argue.

Architecture (per the mini-contract)
sim/ — pure fixed-step engine (60 ticks/game-day), seeded LCG only, weather as a pure function of (seed, day) so forecasts are provably true. Comb cells are the single source of truth for brood and stores; all real beekeeping numbers, annotated with sources in constants.ts
render/ — procedural 2D canvas: palette-driven skies, cached hex-comb bitmaps, additive-glow bee trails, pooled particles (zero alloc in hot paths)
audio.ts — procedural WebAudio: population-scaled hum, filtered-noise wind/rain, event stingers; mute in 5 ms, suspend on pause
loop.ts — the only wall-clock module: fixed-step accumulator, input, journal, toasts, end stats, corrupt-storage-safe persistence
Self-test — 10 headless assertions run at load (including a scripted full-year "good play" run that must reach a WON year — the balance check), surfaced on the title screen
Verified against the checklist
Fresh load renders the title in under a second · three real endings with distinct fail chains (starve/freeze, DWV collapse, queen loss) · drag-slider + mouse + touch + full keyboard · pause freezes sim, particles, and audio context · restart is a clean new seed · tab-blur auto-pauses · resize-safe with DPR clamp · no flash/whiteout · zero network calls — everything (fonts, title art, audio) inlined into one 793 KB dist/index.html

Receipts: 1 AI-generated title-screen painting (inlined, used only on the title); everything else is code and procedure — documented in the README with TRACK: strict-one-shot on line one.

Acqua Alta is a playable cutaway of a Venetian palazzo on the night of 4 November 1966. You stayed for a painting. Elena hid upstairs. The lagoon does not care which of you is the conservator.

Why this had to be a game: breath and a filling staircase cannot be cut into a film. The Madonna’s cost is the seconds you spend in the sala while the stairwell goes under.

Controls: WASD / arrows, Space to jump or kick, E to pick up / smash, Esc pause, M mute.

Built the project
QUOTA — a cold-water commons is built and compiles clean to a single self-contained dist/index.html.

What it is. You chair the Saltworth fishery council for twelve years. Every year you set a total allowable catch for cod and herring, choose an enforcement level, fund town services, and play at most one policy measure — then you watch 300 fishing days play out over an underwater camera while the fleet works.

Why interactive. The tragedy of the commons is a timing failure, not a moral one: this year's catch is cash in hand and next decade's collapse is a projection. You can be told that; you can only feel it by paying restraint's cost yourself, year after year, against a $60M debt at 6%.

The engine (real numbers). Schaefer surplus production dB/dt = rB(1−B/K) − qEB on Atlantic cod (r=0.35, K=62kt) and herring (r=0.85, K=60kt), ex-vessel prices and a blended ~$620/boat-day operating cost from Northwest Atlantic figures. The fleet is 64 boats — deliberately ~1.35× the effort required to take MSY — and every bad thing in the game follows from that. Science gives advice via the real harvest-control rule F = F_MSY·min(1, B/B_MSY) applied to a noisy survey index (σ=0.30, →0.13 with observers); the year-end report then tells you what the biomass actually was. Day rate is tuned so fishing flat out out-earns the advice by ~$1.2M/yr while driving cod from 66% of K toward 20%.

The visuals. Camera under the water. The surface is one plane whose fragment shader calls refract(I, N, 1.333) against analytic wave normals, so Snell's window falls out of the physics rather than being painted; where refract zeros out you get total internal reflection shaded by grazing angle — silvery at the horizon, near-black overhead. Fish and hulls are MeshPhysicalMaterial with clearcoat, iridescence at 420 nm, sheen, and a PMREM environment built from the generated sky. Fourteen additive god-ray billboards, a caustic-lit seabed, 2,200 marine-snow points, then bloom → a grade pass whose desaturation tracks how dead the sea is. The school's visible count is budgeted from real biomass: the water empties as you decide.

Engineering. Fixed 300-step seasons, seeded RNG only, zero top-level side effects, no per-frame allocation, quality settings that touch resolution and effects alone. An 8-check self-test (determinism, logistic recovery to K, F_MSY → MSY, overcapacity → collapse, price elasticity, unbiased/tightened assessment, terminal state) runs on load and reports on the title screen; it passes. Three real fail states — collapse, insolvency, recall — plus graded endings.

Honest notes. I never got to play it, so the magnitude of the greed temptation is derived on paper rather than felt, and the Snell's-window framing is geometry I reasoned about rather than saw (its lower edge sits at 41.4° elevation, the top of frame at ~45°). Both are flagged in the README's self-assessment, along with what I cut (age structure, spatial closures, cod-eats-herring coupling, multi-agent factions) and the full generated-asset receipts — 4 images generated, 3 shipped, each with a procedural fallback so a failed decode degrades instead of breaking.

The game
HONEYDUE — Eight Jars by First Frost. You inherit your grandmother's hive the same season the orchard lease comes due: seal 8 jars of honey in 18 days or Mr. Voss takes the land. It's a real-time systemic defense/economy hybrid built on actual apiculture: split a finite workforce three ways (foragers / guards / nurses — each bee assigned to one is one not doing the others), mark flower patches, plant sunflowers in tilled soil, smoke raids, and treat varroa flares. Wasps escalate on a real biological clock (raids begin day 4, peak in autumn, a hornet queen arrives day 14), guards can heat-ball raiders at 46 °C if you staff the gate, and frost is an immovable deadline. Two fail states (colony collapse / shortfall), three win tiers (Lease Paid → Master Keeper → Golden Comb, which buys the deed back), a story told through Gran's letter and dawn log lines, and day-night-weather-season cycles rendered across one full-bleed meadow.

Engineering
60 Hz fixed-step sim (sim.ts) fully decoupled from rendering; pure top level, seeded RNG only, swap-pop hot loops, durable state in SimState with transient fx events fanned out to audio/renderer/toasts.
Headless self-test runs at boot (and is badged on the title screen): determinism, economy causality, threat pressure, "neglect provably loses," and an 18-day perfect-play bot that must pay the lease.
Procedural WebAudio (swats, splats, heat-ball sizzles, jar chimes, raid alarms, a faint hive hum that dies the frame you mute) — zero audio assets; Bungee + Nunito Variable bundled offline.
Two AI-generated images (meadow horizon band re-tinted per time-of-day, dusk skep key art) — disclosed as receipts in the README; everything else drawn procedurally.
Pause/auto-pause-on-blur, instant mute, resize-safe world-space rendering, corrupt-localStorage-safe persistence, clamped screen flashes.

I have built STRATA-CORE: Thermo-Seismic Drift Rig, a complete, original, 3D exoplanetary mining simulation and sub-surface harvester.

What Was Built & Implemented
Why Interactive? (WHY_INTERACTIVE):
Tectonic extraction is an active balance of opposing physical forces: thermal friction, structural stress, shockwave reflection, and volatile mineral extraction. The player directly manages a 3D thermo-seismic resonance chamber in real time — placing high-yield plasma drills generates deuterium and ignis crystals, but rapidly elevates local core temperatures toward meltdown and creates destructive crustal stress. Survival requires strategically deploying directional heat vents, acoustic dampening pylons, routing liquid coolant, and managing energy budgets across four subterranean depth layers.

Physically-Based 3D Rendering & Materials:

Built using Three.js with WebGL physically-based materials (MeshPhysicalMaterial), incorporating transmission glass (IOR 1.52) for Prism Quartz, iridescence effects for Ignis crystals, metallic specular highlights for Magnetite, and dynamic magma lighting pulses.
Reusable object pools (Vector3, Color) in hot render loops to prevent per-frame garbage collection.
Pure Fixed-Step Simulation Engine (60Hz):

Decoupled from the frame clock using an accumulator loop (dt = 1/60s).
Zero top-level side effects and fully deterministic using seeded Mulberry32 PRNG.
Built-in automated diagnostic self-test (selftest.ts) that verifies simulation invariants on startup.
Complete Game Loop & Progression Systems:

4 Subterranean Depth Layers: Basalt Vault (0-500m), Hydrothermal Mantle (500-1500m), Magma Trench Rift (1500-3500m), Core Singularity Chamber (3500-5000m).
Tactical Hotbar & Placement: Plasma Cutter Drills, Geothermal Heat Vents, Acoustic Dampeners, Volatile Gas Collectors, Emergency Cryo Vent Purge, and Instant Mineral Sales.
Rig Tech Tree & Contracts: Purchase upgrades for radiator panels, sub-harmonic resonators, tungsten drill heads, titanium-graphene frames, and magneto-hydrodynamic reactors.
Real Fail & Victory States: Structural Hull Collapse, Thermal Meltdown, or Tectonic Implosion, with instant reset without page refresh.
Audio & Controls:

Web Audio API procedural sound engine with engine drones, drill lasers, steam vent blasts, alarm sirens, and instant 1-frame mute toggle.
Full mouse orbit controls, zoom controls, and keyboard shortcuts (1-4 for tool selection, Space for pause, M for mute).
