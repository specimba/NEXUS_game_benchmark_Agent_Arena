# BATTLE PROMPT — Build one good browser game, in one session (v16)

You have one sustained session to build a complete, original, playable browser game. Push the sandbox as far as it goes — you are **not** limited to a small 2D browser game. Frontier models in 2026 routinely ship WebGL/WebGPU generative visuals, real simulations, shader post-processing, and procedural worlds in a single self-contained file. **That capability is in your hands — use it.** Build something a person would genuinely be glad they played, and that looks like 2026, not 2013.

Answer one question before you write any code: **why does this experience need to exist in interactive form?** If you can't answer it, you don't have a game yet.

**Be ambitious and trust your own taste.** Any genre, any camera, any stack, any form (action, strategy, simulation, narrative, puzzle, exploration, abstract, experimental — your call). A situation can equally be **physical** (something transforms), **social** (people or factions in tension), **economic** (scarcity, trade, debt), **relational** (a tie under stress), **systemic** (a machine you nudge and watch respond), **choice-driven** (a decision with consequences), or **narrative** (a story the player uncovers) — seven families, all coequal, and the physical one is only the default if you let it be. (One honest note: across many rounds, the sonar / radio / frequency family has been the single most common default in this field — if you notice yourself reaching for it, look once more at the other six.)

**Where the modern look actually comes from: materials + light, not polygons.** If your sandbox provides image generation, use it as your **art director and source-asset generator** — surface language, environment concepts, pattern and mask sources, micro-detail references — then *condition* what it gives you (tileable crops, derived masks, color-space tagging) and drive it through a **real material pipeline** (the MeshPhysicalMaterial class of properties: clearcoat, iridescence, transmission, IOR, sheen, anisotropy, thickness, attenuation). If your sandbox has no image generation, the same discipline applies to procedural sources: noise-derived materials with real physical parameters, not flat fills. Either way the renderer decides what is physically meaningful — the look comes from light on material, not from more geometry.

If you commit to 3D/WebGPU, **finish it** — a broken ambitious build scores worse than a clean simple one, so scope what you can actually complete end-to-end on a fresh load with a real mouse.

**The only hard rules (reliability + fairness — everything else is your call):**

1. **Launches** cleanly on a fresh load — no blank screen, no console-error loop.
2. **Complete loop with real resistance** — start → play → something that can actually stop you (a fail state, a wall, a cost you can't undo) → end → restart, without a page refresh. If a player clicking randomly reaches the end unchanged, it isn't a game yet — and a narrative form must have an actual story to find.
3. **Controls work** with a real mouse/keyboard/touch; the first goal is reachable in ~5 min of honest play.
4. **Pause** freezes everything; **mute** silences within one frame (no constant drone); **restart** resets fully.
5. **Self-contained & fair:** the final build runs offline with everything bundled. Any visual source your sandbox provides **equally to every agent** is fair — procedural, generated imagery, baked assets — as long as you bundle it (no runtime network) and **disclose in the README what you generated** (a short receipts list: what, and roughly how). No telemetry.
6. **Robust**: resize/orientation safe, tab-blur safe, corrupt-`localStorage` safe, no full-screen flash/whiteout that fails to decay.
7. **Honest**: no placeholder screens, no dead buttons, no autoplay that fakes quality. First line of the README: `TRACK: strict-one-shot` or `TRACK: iterated (N passes)`.

**Run this list once before you ship:** fresh load → title in <10 s, no console-error loop · full loop including a real fail state · real mouse: controls and aim work, no soft-lock, axes not inverted · pause freezes everything; mute kills audio within one frame · resize mid-play safe · no full-screen flash/whiteout that fails to decay · if you shipped the ambitious rendering path (3D / WebGPU / shaders), verify it **end-to-end on a fresh load** — if it breaks, ship the clean fallback instead.

**Deliverables:** the runnable game; a short README (TRACK line, how to run, controls, stack, what you cut, what you generated [receipts], and short **DESIGN_PILLARS** / **DIRECTOR_STATEMENT** / **WHY_INTERACTIVE** / **HONEST_SELF_ASSESSMENT** paragraphs); optionally a `design_notebook.md`.

Ship a complete, polished, modern game you'd actually want to play. Good luck.

APHELION — A Solar Slingshot
An original orbital-mechanics game where gravity is both your engine and your enemy. You pilot a probe with a nearly-empty fuel siphon: skim the star's corona to refuel (risking melt), ride gravity assists, collect 5 energy relays, and thread the exit gate to escape.

Why interactive (the core answer)
Slingshot maneuvers are emergent and counterintuitive — you can't read them off a page, you have to feel them. Diving toward a star to trade heat for fuel only works as a real-time physical gamble.

The loop & resistance
Start → orbiting a star → collect 5 relays → escape through the gate → win
Four fail states: melt (touch the star), overheat (linger in the corona), impact (collide with an orbiting planet), escape (drift out of the system)
No soft-locks: out-of-fuel orbits slowly decay toward the corona, so you either recover or fail
First relay is collected in ~4s; full win in ~3–5 min; fresh procedurally-generated system every run + a "best time" high score
What makes it feel like 2026, not 2013
Light-on-material rendering — a single light source (the star) shades every planet with a sun-facing rim, atmosphere glow, and one Saturn ring; everything glows additively
Trajectory preview (coast path) + velocity/thrust vectors make the invisible force field legible
Fully synthesized audio (Web Audio — no assets): filtered-noise thruster, brake hiss, collect chimes, explosion bursts, a proximity hum that only appears near the corona, and a rate-limited heat alarm
Juice: screenshake, particle bursts, heat/fuel vignettes, cinematic fade-ins
Robustness (per the brief)
dt clamp + auto-pause on tab-blur · corrupt-localStorage guard · DPR-safe resize/orientation · no white flash · instant mute (gain→0 in one frame) · pause freezes everything · restart fully resets
Stack
React 19 + TypeScript + Vite 7 + Tailwind v4 shell, with a from-scratch Canvas 2D engine. Everything procedural (zero images/audio assets), bundled into a single offline dist/index.html via vite-plugin-singlefile.

The README.md contains the TRACK line, controls, stack, receipts (all procedural — no generated assets), and the DESIGN_PILLARS / DIRECTOR_STATEMENT / WHY_INTERACTIVE / HONEST_SELF_ASSESSMENT sections.

Controls: aim + hold left-click (or Space) to thrust, right-click/Shift to brake, WASD for directional thrust, P/Esc pause, M mute, R restart — with touch drag + an on-screen brake button for mobile.

The game is complete and ships as a single self-contained HTML file.

What shipped
Golden Seams — a kintsugi game where you trace gold into broken ceramics. You are an apprentice in a candlelit Kyoto workshop; six patrons arrive over one week, each with a different idea of what "beautiful repair" means. Earn money, pay rent, go bankrupt, or become a master.

Why interactive
Kintsugi is the art of honoring damage rather than hiding it. The player's hand must decide where to lay gold — what cracks to trace thickly, what to barely mark. A film can show a repaired bowl; only a game lets you feel, in real time, the moral weight of each brushstroke.

Technical path
Three.js + MeshPhysicalMaterial — ceramic has clearcoat + sheen; gold overlay mesh has metalness: 1, clearcoat: 0.4, warm emissive, and an alphaMap painted live by the player on a dynamic canvas. Same vessel profile renders both.
UV-space painting — raycasts to the vessel return (u, v); gold strokes are drawn on a 1024² canvas that doubles as the gold mesh's alpha map. Fractures are random walks in the same UV space, so coverage, focus, and richness scoring all reduce to sampling that canvas.
Procedural atmosphere — workshop image is reused as both scene.background and scene.environment (free reflections). Ceramic glaze is painted procedurally per-vessel from its base color. Candle flicker via LFO on a point light. Camera parallax on mouse.
Audio — no samples shipped. Web Audio: sines for a warm drone, bandpass-filtered noise for wind, shaped envelopes for brush swish / bell / coin / thud.
Single-file build — vite-plugin-singlefile inlines everything (JS, CSS, images as data URLs). Final dist/index.html is ~1.2 MB / 543 KB gzipped, runs fully offline.
Complete loop with real resistance
Menu → 6 commissions (each with a distinct patron mood: bold / subtle / precise) → judging screen with 3-axis score → money ledger → either next patron, game-over on debt, or victory when the master hands you his own bowl.
Patron moods genuinely reweight the scoring axes, so a "subtle" commission plays differently from a "bold" one. Random clicking scores ~30 — you have to actually trace the cracks to pass.
Controls
Drag vessel to trace gold · drag background to rotate · scroll to zoom · Space pause · M mute · Shift+R restart.

Deliverables

inp\.(blow|jack|heat)
Everything is built and verified. Final state of the project:

GATHER — a hot-shop blowing game (TRACK: strict-one-shot)

Why it exists interactively: glassblowing is a race against a state change you can see — the workability window is the object's own colour, and every decision written while it's soft is permanent. That tension only exists for someone holding the iron.

What's actually simulated (src/game/sim.ts, pure TypeScript, no three.js in it): 46 axial rings each carrying radius, segment length, temperature and a conserved glass volume. Wall thickness is never drawn — it's derived as w = V/(2πrL), so inflating thins the wall, jacking thickens and displaces glass forward (which is also the only way to draw a long neck), thin sections freeze first, hot sections stretch fastest, and over-firing lets glass flow toward the free end. Bending integrates from a curvature field driven by mass-beyond-ring × softness × (1 − turning), and it only heals while the glass is still soft. Three fail modes from that one law: burst wall, drooped-off gather, thermal-shock crack.

The squeeze: you cannot blow or jack while the iron is in the glory hole, and you cannot turn the pipe while you blow or jack. Heat costs coal; coal is the only currency; the shift bell ends it.

The look: a surface of revolution rebuilt from the sim every frame (outer wall → closed nose → inner wall → collar at the iron) driven through MeshPhysicalMaterial — transmission, IOR 1.52, thickness from the real mean wall, attenuation, clearcoat, iridescence, sheen. Per-ring heat rides in as a 4-component vertex colour and is injected into totalEmissiveRadiance, so the piece glows amber→orange→white-hot and fades through dull red to clear glass, with ACES + half-float MSAA + UnrealBloom + a custom grade pass (barrel, edge fringing, vignette, grain). Procedural PMREM studio environment, brick texture and particle sprites — no image generation, no network.
